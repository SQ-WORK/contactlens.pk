Are Your Bella Lenses Original? Counterfeit Detection Guide

Counterfeit Bella, Dahab, and Freshlook lenses flood Pakistani informal markets. And they can cause real eye damage. Here's the 8-point authenticity check every Pakistani buyer should know.

Counterfeit contact lenses are a serious problem in Pakistan. Bella, Dahab, Freshlook, and Acuvue knockoffs flood informal markets at prices 40-70% below authorized retailers. And they cause real eye damage. Unapproved pigments stain corneas. Substandard polymers reduce oxygen flow. Non-sterile packaging introduces microbes.

This guide shows the 8-point authenticity check every Pakistani buyer should perform on contact lens orders, plus the warning signs that something's wrong.

Why Counterfeits Exist (And Why They Matter)

Genuine Bella sells for PKR 5,400 (Elite collection, 3-month wear). Counterfeit Bella in informal markets sells for PKR 1,500-3,000. The economic pressure to undercut authorized prices is real, and unfortunately many small Pakistani sellers source from grey-market distributors who supply mixed legitimate + counterfeit inventory.

Health consequences of wearing counterfeit colored lenses:

  • Cornea staining from unapproved iron oxide pigments. Can be permanent
  • Bacterial keratitis from non-sterile packaging
  • Acanthamoeba infections from contaminated lens material
  • Corneal hypoxia from substandard polymer permeability
  • Permanent reduced vision from chronic exposure to toxic pigments
  • Allergic conjunctivitis from chemical reactions to dyes

Counterfeit transparent lenses (cheap unbranded clear lenses) are even more dangerous because there's no obvious cosmetic clue you're wearing low-quality material.

The 8-Point Authenticity Check

1. Outer Box Branded Packaging

Original Bella, Dahab, Freshlook, and Acuvue always come in branded outer boxes with:

  • Crisp, full-color brand logo
  • Sharp, professional printing (no blurry edges or pixelation)
  • Specific collection name (e.g., "Bella Elite", "Dahab Gold", not just "colored lens")
  • Manufacturing and expiry dates
  • Country of origin (Egypt for Bella, South Korea for Dahab, USA/Ireland for Freshlook)
  • Batch number printed on the box

Counterfeit red flags: generic boxes, blurry printing, missing brand name, no expiry date, "Made in China" for Bella (Bella is made in Egypt only).

2. Holographic Authenticity Sticker

Original Bella has a holographic sticker on the box that shifts colors when tilted. Original Dahab has similar authentication features. Counterfeit packaging often skips the hologram or uses cheap flat stickers that don't shift colors.

Test: Tilt the box under bright light. A genuine hologram changes color/pattern. A fake sticker stays the same.

3. Sealed Blister Pack

Original lenses come in factory-sealed blister packs (small individual foil/plastic packages, one per lens). The seal should be:

  • Perfectly intact with no peeling edges
  • Smooth foil with brand-printed lot/batch info
  • No air bubbles or pressure deformation

Counterfeit red flags: loose seals, missing foil printing, blister packs that look "resealed", visibly damaged packaging.

4. Batch Number on Foil

Original lens blister packs print a batch number directly on the foil (not just on the outer box). The batch number should match the box's batch number. Counterfeits often skip this or print a generic placeholder.

Verify: Bella and Dahab batch numbers can be verified through brand customer service if you're unsure. Don't share these publicly.

5. Original Color Name on Packaging

Original Bella, Dahab, and Freshlook lenses print the specific official color name on the packaging. Examples:

  • "Bella Elite Wild Honey". Not just "honey lens"
  • "Dahab Sun Kiss". Not just "brown lens"
  • "Freshlook ColorBlends Pure Hazel". Not just "hazel"

Counterfeits often use generic descriptors ("brown lens", "grey lens") because they can't reliably replicate the exact branded color name.

6. Dahab Accessories (Specifically for Dahab Orders)

Original Dahab includes specific accessories in every Gold/Platinum box:

  • Complimentary lens solution sachet (small foil packet)
  • Single-use eye drops vial
  • Dahab-branded lens case (gold or platinum themed)
  • Sometimes an authenticity card

Counterfeit Dahab often skips accessories or substitutes with generic unbranded versions.

7. Lens Visual Quality

Once you open the lens, inspect:

  • Pigment uniformity: original colored lens pigment is smooth and consistent. Counterfeits often have pigment "patches" or uneven coverage
  • Lens shape: original lenses sit in a perfect bowl shape on your fingertip. Counterfeits may have warped or asymmetric shapes
  • Lens edge: original lens edges are smooth and rounded. Counterfeit edges can be rough or have visible defects
  • Lens clarity (transparent lenses). Original transparent lenses are perfectly clear. Counterfeits may have a yellowish or cloudy tint

Where Counterfeits Are Common

  • Informal Pakistani markets: street vendors, small kiosks in Khabar Bazaar, Anarkali, Saddar markets
  • Unauthorized Facebook/Instagram sellers: many small operators source from grey markets
  • Daraz / TikTok sellers without verified business credentials
  • Cross-border imports from Lahore Wagah, Karachi port areas
  • "Bulk wholesale" offers at prices significantly below authorized distribution

What to Do If You Suspect You Bought Counterfeits

  1. Stop wearing the lenses immediately if you have any concerns
  2. Check your eyes for symptoms: persistent redness, irritation, blurred vision, sensitivity to light, discharge. Any of these → see an eye doctor
  3. Contact the seller with photos of the suspicious packaging. Legitimate sellers will replace/refund. Counterfeit sellers typically refuse
  4. Report counterfeit operators to brand customer service (Bella has international anti-counterfeit reporting)
  5. Document the receipt and packaging: useful if eye complications develop

Cost Difference Worth The Safety

The price gap between authentic and counterfeit lenses is real (sometimes 50%+) but the eye damage risk is also real. For perspective:

The savings on counterfeit lenses don't justify even a small risk of permanent eye damage. Buy authentic from authorized sources.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I know if my Bella contact lenses are original?
Check 8 things on your Bella lenses: (1) Branded outer box with crisp Bella logo, (2) Holographic authenticity sticker that shifts colors when tilted, (3) Factory-sealed blister pack with no peeling, (4) Batch number printed on the foil, (5) Specific color name (e.g., 'Bella Elite Wild Honey' not just 'honey lens'), (6) Made in Egypt origin (Bella isn't made in China), (7) Lens pigment is smooth and uniform when inspected, (8) Price within market range (Bella Elite is PKR 5,400. Significantly below market = suspicious).
What happens if I wear counterfeit contact lenses?
Counterfeit colored lenses can cause cornea staining from unapproved iron oxide pigments (sometimes permanent), bacterial keratitis from non-sterile packaging, Acanthamoeba infections from contaminated lens material, corneal hypoxia from substandard polymer, and allergic conjunctivitis from chemical dyes. In severe cases, counterfeit lens use has caused permanent vision reduction and required corneal transplants. The cost of treatment far exceeds any savings from buying counterfeits.
Why are counterfeit contact lenses cheaper?
Counterfeit lenses are cheaper because they skip safety standards: unapproved pigments cost a fraction of FDA-approved ones, substandard polymers reduce material costs, non-sterile packaging eliminates clean room manufacturing costs, and unauthorized distribution bypasses brand licensing fees. The economic pressure to undercut authorized prices is real. But the cost reduction comes from removing the very things that make lenses safe to wear in your eyes.
Where can I buy authentic Bella, Dahab, and Freshlook lenses in Pakistan?

What should I do if I think I bought counterfeit lenses?
