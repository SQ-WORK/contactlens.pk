What Are Eyesight Lenses?

"Eyesight lenses" (also called prescription contact lenses, eye lenses for weak eyesight, or power lenses) are contact lenses that correct refractive errors. The same conditions glasses correct. The three main eyesight problems and the lens type each needs:

  • Myopia (nearsightedness): far objects blurry. Corrected by negative-power lenses (-0.50, -1.00, -2.00 and so on, down to -10.00 or higher). Most Pakistani buyers have myopia.
  • Hyperopia (farsightedness): near objects blurry. Corrected by positive-power lenses (+0.50, +1.00, etc.).
  • Astigmatism: blurry vision at all distances due to irregular cornea shape. Corrected by Toric lenses requiring SPH + CYL + AXIS values.

How to Read Your Eye Prescription

Your prescription card from the optometrist will have several values:

ValueWhat It Means
SPH (Sphere)Main power. Negative for myopia, positive for hyperopia
CYL (Cylinder)Astigmatism power. If present, you need Toric lenses
AXISDirection of astigmatism (1° to 180°)
BC (Base Curve)Lens curvature. Usually 8.4mm, 8.5mm, or 8.6mm
DIA (Diameter)Lens size. Typically 14.0mm to 14.5mm
ADDReading addition power (for multifocal lenses)

If you only see SPH and BC/DIA values, you need standard spherical lenses (any product in this hub). If you also see CYL and AXIS, you need a Toric lens (Biomedics Toric or Acuity Toric).

Safety Tips for First-Time Eyesight Lens Wearers

  • Get a fitting first. Visit an optometrist to confirm your eye shape suits the lens base curve and diameter. Wrong fit causes discomfort and red eyes.
  • Start with monthly disposables. Lower cost than daily disposables but easier to maintain than yearly lenses while you learn handling.
  • Never wear lenses while sleeping unless they're specifically labeled extended wear (Acuvue Oasys).
  • Never use water on lenses. Tap water and bottled water both contain microbes that cause serious eye infections.
  • Replace your case every 3 months. Even clean cases develop biofilm.
  • If your eyes get red or sore, remove lenses immediately and consult an optometrist. Don't push through discomfort.
